    Atlas of Indigenous Peoples

    ✦

    The accounts of the travelers, missionaries, traders and settlers of French America speak constantly of the peoples among whom they lived, traded, married or fought. This atlas rereads all the sources of the Atlas of French America from the point of view of the First Nations, the Métis and the Inuit: villages, councils, treaties, wars, alliances and named individuals, each entry with its date, place, nation, source and a link to the page.

    Click a point to open its entry; the Nations filter groups the nations by major cultural area.

    English version: the interface is translated; the entries are being translated in batches, and those not yet translated are shown in the original French.

    The atlases of the network

    Please read before browsing

    The sources were almost all written by Europeans and Euro-Americans, from the seventeenth to the twentieth century. They use period names for nations and period terms (“sauvages,” “squaw,” “half-breed”…) that are often pejorative; the atlas quotes them as they are, to remain faithful to the texts, and gives for each nation the name in use today. The view of Indigenous peoples is that of the authors, not that of the nations themselves.

    Nations and personal names were identified in the text of the entries; no name has been added. When the text does not name the nation, the entry is classified as “Indigenous (nation not specified).”

    About the atlas

    A geohistorical atlas of the First Nations, Métis and Inuit as they appear in the sources of French America, from New France to the twentieth century: villages and territories, councils, treaties and conflicts, missions, trade, alliances and marriages, guides and interpreters, and the Indigenous people named in the texts.

    Sources

    All the entries of the Atlas of the French Canadians of the West (Cragin's notebooks, Tassé, Hafen, French Illinois, New France, Louisiana, the archive.org library of more than two hundred books, etc.) and of the Aubry, Iberville, Jolliet and Robidoux atlases were reread. Each entry keeps the reference and link of its source (page of the book on archive.org, PDF of the issue, article) and points back to the original entry.

    Reading the entries

    Each nation is given under its current name, with period names in parentheses (Sioux, Chickasaw, Flathead…); an entry may concern several nations. Dates are expressed in decimal years for filtering and in EDTF; certainty refers to the location. The descriptions are those of the original entries, translated from the French; entries not yet translated are shown in French.

    The period terms used by the sources are reproduced as they are; they may be hurtful.
